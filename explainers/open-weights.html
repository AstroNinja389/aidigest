What does "open weights" mean?

The short version: an AI model with "open weights" is one whose finished internals are published, so anyone can download it and run it on their own computers instead of only using it through the company's website.

What the weights are

An AI model is, in the end, an enormous list of numbers. Training is the slow, expensive process of adjusting those numbers until the model gives good answers. The finished numbers are called the "weights". If you have them, you have the model.

Open versus closed

Most of the best-known chatbots are "closed": the company keeps the weights secret and lets you use the model only through its own app or service. An "open-weights" model is handed out like a recipe anyone can cook. People can run it privately, adapt it to their own work, and study how it behaves.

Why it is debated

Supporters say open models let researchers check claims for themselves and stop a few companies controlling the technology. Critics point out that once the weights are published they cannot be taken back, and any safety limits can be removed by whoever downloads them. That is why companies often run outside safety tests before releasing weights.
