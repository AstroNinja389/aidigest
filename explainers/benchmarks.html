What is a benchmark?

The short version: a benchmark is a fixed test, a set of questions or tasks with known right answers, that researchers give to many AI systems so they can compare the scores. It works like a standard exam.

Why they matter

When a company says its new AI is "better", it usually means it scored higher on some benchmarks. Because everyone uses the same test, the numbers can be compared across companies and over time.

Why a high score can mislead

Benchmarks wear out. Once a test is well known, AI systems get very good at that particular test, sometimes partly because similar questions ended up in what they learned from. Scores near 100% then say little about how the AI copes with real, messy work. Benchmark tasks are also usually neat and self-contained, while real jobs involve unclear requests, changing instructions and many steps.

What researchers do about it

They keep building harder or more realistic tests, for example from real requests that real people made, and they check whether the AI finished the whole task, not just part of it. Who runs the test matters too: results from a company testing its own AI deserve more caution than results from an outside group (see why it matters who tests the AI).
