What is a preprint?

The short version: a preprint is a scientific paper that the authors have posted publicly before other scientists have formally checked it. It is a first draft shared early, not a verdict.

How papers are normally checked

Usually a paper goes to a journal, which sends it to a few independent experts. They look for mistakes, ask for more experiments, and recommend whether to publish it. This is called peer review, and it can take months.

Why scientists post preprints

Waiting months slows everyone down, so many researchers also post their paper on a free public website (for biology, the best known is bioRxiv, pronounced "bio-archive"). Others can read it, try to repeat it, and point out problems straight away. Most preprints are later submitted to a journal.

How to read one

A preprint can be excellent, and many turn out to be right. But nobody outside the team has been formally asked to check it yet. So the honest way to describe a preprint's result is "the authors report", not "scientists have shown".
