Why it matters who tests the AI

The short version: when a company tests its own product, the result may be perfectly honest, but it is worth knowing that it is the company's own result.

An everyday comparison

A food company can print nutrition facts on its own packaging. An independent laboratory can also test the food. Usually they agree. But if only one party ever does the measuring, nobody else can check it, and small choices, such as what to measure and what to leave out, can quietly shape the story.

How this applies to AI

Companies that build AI models have the most access to them and often the most expertise, so they run many of the studies. Outside groups have less access but no stake in the answer. A healthy picture usually has both. When a study is run by the maker, it helps to ask what they chose to publish, whether the details are open enough for others to check, and whether an independent group got a similar result.

What this does not mean

It does not mean company studies are wrong, or that outside ones are always right. Everyone has limits. The point is just to notice who is talking.
