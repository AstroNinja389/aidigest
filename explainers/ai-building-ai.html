AI helping to build AI

The short version: the people who build AI now use AI as part of their own work. The open question is how much faster that makes progress, and who can check the answer.

The feedback loop

Programmers at AI companies use AI to write code, run experiments and check results, just as programmers elsewhere do. If the tool makes them faster, the next generation of AI arrives sooner, which can make them faster still. People call this a feedback loop. Nobody doubts the loop exists. The argument is about how strong it is.

What an outside group measured

METR is an independent nonprofit that evaluates AI. It spent about eleven weeks (12 August to 29 October 2026) studying internal data at Anthropic and published its report on 5 November. Its best estimate is that Anthropic's models are now improving at well-defined tasks about twice as fast as they did between March 2024 and October 2025. Put another way, about two years of earlier progress now happens in one. METR says the true figure could plausibly lie anywhere from about 1.3 to 3 times.

METR also estimates, as a rough judgment, that around 80% of the speedup comes from AI helping with the research itself, rather than from simply using more computing power.

Why the report comes with big warnings

It measures test scores. Progress was tracked mainly through scores on benchmarks, which are standardised tests for AI. These can miss real-world ability, and they are easy to over-fit.

Limited access. METR did not get to query unreleased models, and it leaned heavily on two Anthropic staff members.

Most of the key evidence was removed. Anthropic had the right to take out confidential material and removed most of what METR found most informative. METR says this means readers have to rely on its judgment rather than check the evidence themselves.

A planned check was cancelled. A review of how Anthropic divides its computing power between making models more capable and making them safer was dropped partway through, so METR could not verify it.

The warning line

According to coverage by The Decoder, Anthropic's own safety policy treats a doubling of speed as a warning signal. Its public report in August said the figure was "less than a factor of 2", while an internal figure was 1.97, and METR thinks the line may have been crossed as early as mid-July. Both sides, as reported, agree the immediate risk of catastrophe is currently low.

What it does not say

It does not show that AI is building itself. People still decide what to build, how to test it and when to release it. Employees' own estimates of how much faster they work ranged widely, and METR calls the highest ones very loose evidence.

A disclosure

This page was written by Claude, which is made by Anthropic, the company in the report. METR says it also used Claude heavily in its own analysis. That is one reason to read METR's original report as well as any summary of it.
