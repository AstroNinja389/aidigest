How scientists use AI

The short version: AI is good at finding patterns in more data than a person could ever go through, and at speeding up routine work. It does not replace the scientist's job of deciding what the pattern means.

Three common uses

Finding patterns in huge piles of data. Researchers studying dolphins have decades of recordings. Listening to all of it by hand is impossible, but software can search for sounds that repeat in similar situations.

Searching images. Satellites photograph the whole Earth, again and again. AI can let a person ask in everyday language to see, for example, where forests have shrunk over ten years.

Speeding up lab routines. AI can help write the small programs, analyse results, or plan steps that otherwise eat up days.

What AI does not do

Finding a pattern is not the same as understanding it. If software finds a repeating dolphin sound, a scientist still has to work out what, if anything, it means. In the dolphin case, no genuine two-way conversation has been shown. AI can also make confident mistakes, so results need checking.
