Does each neuron mean one thing?

The short version: sometimes, which is why the idea is tempting. But often a single neuron is a messy mixture of unrelated things.

The tempting picture

The simplest hope is one neuron per idea: a "dog ear" neuron, a "Paris" neuron, a "sadness" neuron. If that were true, understanding an AI would be like reading a labelled switchboard.

Neuroscientists have wondered the same about brains for a long time, asking whether single brain cells might respond to single concepts.

Some neurons really do look like that

In 2021, OpenAI researchers studying an image-and-text system reported neurons that responded to one idea in many forms. One fired for Spider-Man, whether it saw a photo, a drawing, or even the written word. A news summary of that finding is a good short read.

But many are mixtures

Many neurons are not like that. Researchers have found neurons in image models that respond to, for example, both cat faces and the fronts of cars. Researchers call a neuron like that polysemantic, meaning "many meanings". A neuron that stands for one thing is monosemantic.

If most neurons are mixtures, then looking at one neuron tells you little. You cannot read the switchboard. That raises a puzzle: why would a network learn such a tangled arrangement?
