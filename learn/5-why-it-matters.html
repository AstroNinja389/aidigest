Inside AI · Lesson 5 of 5

Why this matters, and what is still unsolved

The short version: today we mostly judge an AI by what it says. Interpretability tries to check what it is doing inside.

Why look inside at all

Imagine judging a person's honesty only from their words. You can learn a lot, but a skilled liar passes. If we could see the inner workings, we could ask different questions: is the model relying on a shortcut? Is it behaving well only because it knows it is being tested? Today those are mostly guesses from outside.

Features also offer a way to steer, as the Golden Gate demo showed, and to monitor, by watching whether a worrying feature switches on.

What is still unsolved

Completeness. We can find some features, but no one can yet say we have found all the relevant ones.

Faithfulness. Does the description match what the model really relies on, or is it a convenient story?

Scale. The models keep growing and changing, and the work has to keep up.

Independence. Much of this research comes from the companies building the models. See why it matters who tests the AI.

A question to think about

If we could read an AI's inner workings reliably, what would you want checked before relying on it? That question, put more precisely, is what many researchers in this field are working on.
