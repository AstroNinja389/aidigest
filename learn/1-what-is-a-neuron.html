What is a "neuron" in an AI?

The short version: an artificial neuron is a very simple calculator. A large AI is made of millions or billions of them wired together.

What one neuron does

A neuron receives several numbers. It decides how much to care about each one (that "how much" is called a weight), adds them up, and passes on a single number. If the total is high enough, the signal it passes on is strong. If not, it stays quiet.

That is all. One neuron cannot recognise a face or write a sentence. The power comes from enormous numbers of them, arranged in layers, each layer feeding the next.

Nobody writes the weights

This is the surprising part. Engineers do not set the weights by hand. The network starts with random weights and is shown a vast amount of example text or images. Each time it gets something wrong, the weights are nudged slightly. After billions of nudges, the network works, but nobody wrote down why.

Why the name

The term is borrowed from brain cells, which also receive signals and pass them on. Real brain cells are far more complicated. The comparison is an inspiration, not a description.

Why that matters

Because nobody designed the inner workings, nobody knows what they are. Studying a trained AI is closer to a biologist studying an animal than an engineer reading a blueprint. That is the problem interpretability tries to solve.
